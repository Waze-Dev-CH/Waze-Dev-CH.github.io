# Getting started as a new editor

Welcome! You have just started editing the Waze map in Switzerland, or you are about to. This page gathers the essentials to get off on the right foot: understanding your level, opening the editor, installing the right tool, and knowing what to change without risk.

::: note It's normal to move carefully when starting out
Level 1 (L1) is the discovery level. Nobody expects perfect edits from you on day one. What matters: make small safe edits, read before acting, and ask when in doubt.
:::

## Understanding the L1 level

Every new editor starts at level 1. This level defines what you can edit and where.

| | Level 1 |
| --- | --- |
| Editing radius | 1.6 km around your trips |
| Minimum edits | 0 (automatic assignment) |
| Locking | you can edit segments locked at level 1 |
| Duration | driving-related rights expire after 3 months of inactivity |

The move to level 2 is automatic once the criteria are met. For all the details on levels and roles, see [Editor levels](/en/editors/wme/niveaux) and [Editor roles](/en/editors/wme/roles).

## Opening the editor (WME)

Edits are made in the **Waze Map Editor (WME)**, available at [waze.com/editor](https://www.waze.com/editor) with your Waze account.

Take the time to get familiar with the interface before changing anything. Your editing area is located around the trips you make with the app: start with a place you know well, like your neighborhood.

## Installing the right tool

::: help WME Switzerland Helper
Install the userscript [**WME Switzerland Helper**](https://github.com/Waze-Dev-CH/WME-Switzerland-Helper) right from the start. It adds the Swiss geographic layers, public transport data (SBB stops) and a street-name check, all valuable aids for editing according to local rules.
:::

The script is installed via a userscript manager such as Tampermonkey. Details and other tools on the [Resources](/en/editors/communaute/ressources) page.

## The trap to absolutely avoid

::: important Never apply a neighboring country's rules
Switzerland has its own editing rules. A practice that is valid in France, Germany or Italy is **not necessarily** valid here, even if we share a border and a language. Never apply a rule by simple analogy.
:::

The right reflex: read the Wiki and the forum before editing, and ask when unsure.

::: success One more question is better
One more question is always better than an edit that needs fixing. Ask it on the [community Discord](https://discord.gg/dmxUwvTkk5) or the [Romandie forum](https://www.waze.com/forum/viewforum.php?f=836).
:::

## What can I safely edit at L1?

For your first edits, stick to simple, low-risk changes:

- correct a street name according to Swiss conventions (see [Naming roads and cities](/en/editors/cartographie/nommage-routes));
- slightly adjust the geometry of a segment you know on the ground;
- handle simple reports.

::: failure Keep for later
At first, avoid topics that require experience and often a higher level: complex intersections, [roundabouts](/en/editors/cartographie/ronds-points), [lanes](/en/editors/wme/lanes-passages-niveau) (L3+) or [permanent hazards](/en/editors/wme/dangers-permanents) (L4+). Watch how experienced editors do it, and ask before diving in.
:::

## Getting started checklist

1. Have an active Waze account (use the app on your trips).
2. Open the [Waze Map Editor](https://www.waze.com/editor) and explore the [interface](/en/editors/wme/interface).
3. Understand your [L1 level and your editing rights](/en/editors/wme/niveaux).
4. Install the **WME Switzerland Helper** userscript (see [Scripts and tools](/en/editors/wme/scripts)).
5. Read the basics: [Road naming](/en/editors/cartographie/nommage-routes), [Map legend](/en/editors/wme/legende-carte) and [Routing basics](/en/editors/wme/routing).
6. Read the [Swiss editing rules](/en/editors/cartographie/regles-edition).
7. Join the Romandie forum and Discord (see [Community](/en/editors/communaute/)).
8. Make a first small simple edit, in an area you know.
